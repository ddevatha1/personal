Project 4: Wish List Manager

Curriculum Day 25 of 4024 students completed itDeeptanshu Devatha

Wish List Manager is Project 4, on Day 25 of Year 1. It follows a week on lists: indexing, slicing, list methods, looping over lists, and turning sentences into word lists. The curriculum asks students to design the menu on paper first and then test it in pairs.

What I taught that week

From the Year 1 curriculum, Week 5: Lists & Tuples

Day 21: Lists: Organizing Many Things. Creating lists with [], zero-based indexing, negative indexes, len(), and slicing with [start:end]. Comparing lists with strings. Practice: "Top 5 Ranker".

Day 22: List Methods & Mutability. Changing lists in place with .append(), .insert(), .remove(), .pop(), .sort(), and .reverse(). Membership tests with in and not in. Practice: "Playlist Editor".

Day 23: Looping Over Lists & Tuples. Using for item in my_list, building new lists in a loop, and finding totals, maximums, and minimums. Tuples as read-only lists, and unpacking like x, y = point.

Day 24: Words in a List. Turning text into lists with .split() and back into text with .join(). Sorting words and finding the longest word. Lab: "Sentence Scrambler" that shuffles a sentence.

Day 25: Lab: Wish List Manager. Project 4. A menu-driven program that combines while, input(), if/elif, and lists to add, remove, and show items. Design the menu on paper first, then test in pairs.

What students built

24 students built a list manager. Every one of them can add and remove items and checks whether an item is already on the list before adding it again; 46% added a sort option. The median program is 50.5 lines. The themes vary (reading lists, grocery lists and more); all 24 submissions are different variants.

One student's program

This is one real submission, shown as the student wrote it apart from the removed name header. It was chosen by a fixed rule rather than for being the best: it is the submission closest to the project's median length among those that ran cleanly and contain nothing that could identify anyone. Parts are skipped where marked with …

while True:
    print()
    print("[A] Add a grocery item")
    print("[R] Remove a grocery item")
    print("[S] Show my grocery list")
    print("[O] Sort A-Z")
    print("[C] Clear everything")
    print("[Q] Quit")
    choice = input("Command: ").lower().strip()
    if choice == 'a':
        item = input("Which grocery item do you want to add? ")
        if item in items:
            print(item + " is already on your grocery list!")
        else:
            items.append(item)
            print(f"Added {item}. Total: {len(items)}")
    elif choice == 'r':
        item = input("Which grocery item should I remove? ")
        if item in items:
            items.remove(item)
            print(f"Removed {item}.")
        else:
            print("I could not find that one.")
    elif choice == 's':
        if len(items) == 0:
            print("Your grocery list is empty.")
        else:
            print("--- Your grocery list ---")
            number = 1
            for item in items:
                print(f"{number}. {item}")
                number += 1
    elif choice == 'o':
        items.sort()
        print("Sorted your grocery list from A to Z.")
    elif choice == 'c':
        sure = input("Really clear everything? (yes/no) ").lower()
        if sure == "yes":
            items = []
            print("All clear!")
        else:
            print("Okay, I kept everything.")
    elif choice == "q":
        print(goodbye)
        break
    else:
        print("Sorry, I do not know that command.")
