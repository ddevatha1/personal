X-Step: a plantar-pressure insole and app

X-Step is a student team's four-sensor insole for people at risk of diabetic foot problems. It streams foot pressure over Bluetooth to a phone app that shows live loading, trends and alerts.

My role: co-founder; machine-learning and mobile lead

  1. System
  2. ML pipeline
  3. Status
  4. Recognition

System, end to end

  • Insole. Four FSR402 force sensors under the first, second and fifth metatarsal heads and the heel, one board per foot.
  • Firmware. An ESP32 samples each sensor at 25 Hz and advertises as XSTEP-L or XSTEP-R.
  • Link. A 28-byte Bluetooth Low Energy frame carries four ADC readings, a timestamp and a sequence counter, so dropped packets are detected rather than hidden.
  • Features and model. On the host side, rolling 4-second windows are turned into 59 pressure and gait features and scored by a logistic-regression model.
  • App. A React Native (Expo) app pairs with both insoles and shows live pressure, cadence, trends and alerts, with a separate clinician view.

ML pipeline

My main technical contribution is the feature pipeline and model. Per site, the pipeline computes peak and mean pressure, the coefficient of variation and the pressure–time integral. Per window, it computes cadence, stance ratio and anterior load share. A 540-parameter logistic-regression head runs on these features. The whole path takes well under a millisecond per window, so it fits between 40 ms samples.

We've kept the claims deliberately narrow. The team's paper evaluates the insole as a measurement instrument: calibration error, which features survive sensor drift, and what four sensors can and can't recover compared with a 32-sensor insole. It makes no ulcer-prediction claim. That analysis is on the X-Step research page.

Status

X-Step is a working prototype. No walking data from X-Step wearers have been collected yet. A prospective study under ethics approval is the step between this prototype and any clinical use.
