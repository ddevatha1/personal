Mad Libs Machine

Combining input(), variables, and f-strings into a complete program: a silly story with blanks that players fill in. Pair programming with driver and navigator roles.

Where it falls

Week 2, Words As Data, Day 6 of 40 in the 2024 curriculum (Python from zero).

What students learned

Combine input(), variables and f-strings into one complete program.

Concepts

input(); variables; f-strings; pair programming (driver/navigator).

What it asks of students

To finish this project a student has to bring the ideas above together in one working program that runs from start to finish.

The lessons that lead into it

Day 1: Welcome to Coding & Setup. What a program is, and the Input → Process → Output idea. Installing Python with the Thonny editor and touring the editor and the Shell. Writing a first program: print("Hello, KidCode!").

Day 2: Print, Escapes & Comments. Mastering print(), fixing quote errors, and using escape characters (\n, \t, \'). Adding # comments. Lab: "Name Banner" ASCII art.

Day 3: Variables: Labeled Boxes. Storing information with =. Variable naming rules and snake_case, changing values, and checking data types with type() (str, int, float). Lab: "About Me Card" built from variables.

Day 4: Number Crunching. Arithmetic operators (+, -, *, /, //, %, **) and PEMDAS. Integers vs. floats, and the TypeError from mixing text and numbers. Lab: "Snack Shop Calculator".

Day 5: Talking to the User. Asking questions with input(), converting answers with int() and float(), and formatting output with f-strings like f"Hi, {name}!". Practice: "Age in Dog Years" and "Tip Helper".

How it is assessed

The project sits inside pre/post assessment pair 1 of 4 for 2024, “Python Basics & Strings” (Weeks 1–2 (Days 1–10)). More on the assessments.
