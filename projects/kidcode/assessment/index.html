What changed

There are 13 pairs of assessments across the three years: 4 in 2024, 5 in 2025 and 4 in 2026. Students take them on paper, in about 45 minutes, without a computer. They ask students to predict what a program prints, name an error, or fix or write short code.

Of the 104 students who took both a pre- and a post-assessment, the average score went from 41.7% to 58.8%, a gain of 17.1 percentage points (a 41.0% relative increase). 99 students improved, 4 stayed the same and 1 went down.

Notes & context

  • The results show how scores changed between the pre- and post-assessment. There was no comparison group, so they don't measure what caused the change.
  • Each student has one before and one after percentage. I don't have the unit-by-unit scores, so results can't be broken down by unit.
  • Averages are grouped by the year a student first joined. 104 of the 124 students took both assessments.
  • The relative increase is the change divided by the starting average. The 95% interval for the average gain is 15.3 to 18.9 percentage points.
