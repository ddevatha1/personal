Predicting Cognitive Function Using Transformer-Derived Speech Representations and Longitudinal Coherence Features
May 2025–Present

First Author·Mentor: Joe Xiao (PhD student)·CRediT: conceptualization, methodology, software, formal analysis, validation, original draft

medRxiv preprint (Aug. 2026)·Under review, Frontiers in Aging Neuroscience·Public code·26-entry research log

Question

Can transcribed speech plus the clinical history already on file forecast a patient’s next-visit MMSE (0–30)? Framed as longitudinal, patient-level supervised regression — forecasting, where most prior speech work does single-visit classification.

Data

DementiaBank Pitt Corpus (controlled access): 126 patients (69 dementia, 57 control), 2–5 visits each → 173 prediction samples, each mapping 1–4 prior visits to a later-visit MMSE.

Features

Designed a 53-dimensional feature vector: 12 per-visit features — Sentence-BERT discourse coherence (adjacent-sentence cosine similarity, global coherence, semantic slope, topic drift, embedding variance) plus spaCy lexical/syntactic measures (filler rate, 50-token MATTR, inter-sentence repetition, POS-based fragment ratio, named-entity rate) — with 8 visit-to-visit Δ features, 4 temporal features, 20 PCA components of 384-d SBERT embeddings, and 9 clinical/demographic covariates.

Model

LightGBM, chosen for small mixed-type tabular data: RobustScaler → correlation pruning (|r| > 0.8, clinical variables kept) → permutation-importance selection (top 15) → quantile binning → 3× Gaussian augmentation (2% of feature SD) tagged by parent patient → Optuna TPE over 10 hyperparameters, 200 trials, maximizing mean patient-grouped R².

Validation

Diagnosed patient-identity leakage: folds split by visit, so a model could score by recognizing a person. Rebuilt evaluation as 5-fold GroupKFold on patient ID, locking augmented copies to their parent’s fold and scoring only out-of-fold predictions on original samples; scores fell, as they should once leaked identity is removed.

Debugging

Traced why entry MMSE — the expected strongest predictor — contributed almost nothing to two silent data-join bugs (a renamed column; a string-vs-integer patient-ID mismatch) that imputed one value for every patient; the fix was the project’s largest single improvement.

Results

R² 0.840 ± 0.015·RMSE 2.75·MAE 2.02 MMSE points·Pearson r 0.917 — error comparable to MMSE inter-rater variability. Per-fold permutation importance (10 repeats): entry MMSE, baseline diagnosis and Blessed score lead; SBERT PC1 ranks third.

Ablation

Controlled feature-source ablation (fixed untuned LightGBM, same folds): linguistic-only R² 0.118, SBERT-only 0.458, clinical-only 0.716, clinical + SBERT 0.759. Clinical history dominates; SBERT beats handcrafted features and adds directional, complementary signal (fold SDs overlap). Dropping Δ features changed nothing (0.740), overturning an earlier claim.

Integrity & limits

Audited the manuscript line by line against the code, removing six coreference/entity features the model never used. Limits: non-nested selection and tuning (CV may be optimistic); one cohort, no external validation; over-prediction below MMSE 10.

Paper 2 · X-Step research paper · Biomedical sensing
A Four-Site Plantar-Pressure Insole: Transducer Error Budget, Feature Equivariance, and Sparse-Versus-Dense Agreement in Overground Walking
2025–Present

2nd of 5 authors · ML lead·MIT CSAIL first author; UC Santa Cruz AIEA Lab (Prof. Leilani Gilpin)·Under review, IEEE-EMBS IECBES 2026

Framing

Evaluates the team’s four-FSR402 insole (MET1, MET2, MET5, heel; ESP32, 25 Hz, BLE) as a measurement instrument, not a clinical classifier. My contributions: the ML pipeline and the sparse-vs-dense correlation study; the hardware was team work.

ML pipeline

Designed the feature map from rolling 4 s windows (100 samples × 8 channels) to 59 features — per-site peak, mean, coefficient of variation and pressure–time integral; window-level cadence, stance ratio, anterior load share, aggregate peak and total PTI — feeding a 540-parameter logistic-regression head (531 multiply–accumulates; ≈0.23 ms per update at 25 Hz).

Correlation study

Designed a sparse-vs-dense study on an independent 32-cell insole archive (149 recordings, 15 adults, 298 foot-takes), comparing the cell nearest each X-Step site with its anatomical cluster maximum. Shape survived (median r 0.72–0.99), magnitude did not (peak-to-peak r 0.43–0.74); four-site center of pressure tracked anteroposterior (r 0.81) but not mediolateral motion (r 0.01) — a geometric limit of a layout with no medial–lateral pair. Reported as descriptive (non-independent foot-takes, no p-values); Pearson r certifies shape, not magnitude.

Evaluation

Quantified subject-identity leakage on a synthetic 24-subject, 9-class cohort: macro-F1 0.885 subject-grouped vs 0.931 ungrouped (majority baseline 0.040) — a transfer gap larger than the spread across all classifiers tested. Peak-only features matched the full map (0.886), read as peak-separable synthetic labels, not proof the full map is needed.

Scope

No walking data from X-Step wearers yet; findings are engineering-level, with no ulcer-prediction or clinical-benefit claim.
