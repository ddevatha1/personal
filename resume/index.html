Research

Predicting Cognitive Function Using Transformer-Derived Speech Representations and Longitudinal Coherence Features — First AuthorMay 2025–Present

Mentor: Joe Xiao (PhD student) · Under peer review, Frontiers in Aging Neuroscience — submitted August 04, 2026 · Preprint medrxiv.org/content/10.64898/2026.08.04.26359730v1

  • Question: can a patient's speech across clinic visits forecast their next Mini-Mental State Exam (MMSE) score? Framed as supervised regression on the DementiaBank Pitt Corpus (173 longitudinal samples, 126 patients) using only earlier visits.
  • Representation: to capture how discourse organization changes with decline, extracted 12 per-visit linguistic features (POS fragment ratio, named-entity rate, 50-token MATTR, filler rate, perseveration) and Sentence-BERT coherence features (adjacent-sentence cosine similarity, global coherence, semantic slope, topic drift, embedding variance), plus visit-to-visit Δ features.
  • Model: reduced 384-d SBERT embeddings to 20 PCA components, added 9 clinical/demographic covariates (53 dimensions), and tuned LightGBM with Optuna/TPE over 200 trials (RobustScaler, correlation pruning, permutation-importance selection).
  • Evaluation: found patient-level leakage (one patient on both sides of a split) and rebuilt evaluation with patient-grouped 5-fold GroupKFold, augmented copies kept in their patient's fold, original samples only scored: R² 0.840 ± 0.015, RMSE 2.75, MAE 2.02 MMSE points. Ablation: clinical history carries most of the signal (clinical-only R² 0.72, SBERT-only 0.46, linguistic-only 0.12); speech adds a smaller gain (0.76 combined); non-nested tuning is disclosed as an optimism limit. A Four-Site Plantar-Pressure Insole: Transducer Error Budget, Feature Equivariance, and Sparse-Versus-Dense Agreement in Overground Walking — 2nd of 5 Authors, ML LeadJul. 2026–Present

UC Santa Cruz AIEA Lab (Prof. Leilani Gilpin) aiea-lab.github.io · deeptanshudevatha.com/research/x-step

  • Question: can four strategically placed sensing sites capture regional pressure trends comparable to a 32-sensor insole? I engineered the machine-learning component: a 59-feature representation of the team’s four-site plantar-pressure readings (per-site peak/mean/CV, pressure–time integral, cadence, stance ratio, anterior load share) and a 540-parameter logistic-regression framework that classifies biomechanical risk.
  • Designed the correlation study (149 recordings, 15 adults, public 32-cell insole): four sites keep waveform shape (median r = 0.72–0.99) but not magnitude (peak-to-peak r = 0.43–0.74); center-of-pressure tracking keeps anteroposterior motion (r = 0.81) but loses mediolateral (r = 0.01).
  • Quantified subject-identity leakage on a synthetic 24-subject cohort (macro-F1 0.885 subject-grouped vs 0.931 ungrouped) and documented the generalization limit.
